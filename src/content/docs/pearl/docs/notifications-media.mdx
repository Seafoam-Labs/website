The bar's `notifications`, `tray` and `media` widgets provide Pearl's session
services: desktop notifications with a grouped history, application tray icons
with their menus, and player controls for your music and video applications.
All three are optional bar widgets, added and arranged in **Settings → Bar &
dock**, and each opens its own popup from the bar.

## Notifications

New notifications appear as toast cards on the focused output, up to three at
a time and fewer on short displays. Toasts take no keyboard focus and never
reserve screen space; they accept clicks, move aside while another Pearl popup
is open, and disappear after at most eight seconds. A notification may carry
action buttons, and a card whose sender registered a default action runs it
when you press the card itself.

The notifications button in the bar opens the history, grouped by application
and ordered by newest delivery, with **Do Not Disturb** and **Clear History**
at the top. History lives in memory for the current Pearl process only; it is
never written to disk, holds at most 64 records, and evicts the oldest
inactive records first. Clear History keeps still-active notifications. An
application that updates a notification replaces its card and history entry
in place.

- **Do Not Disturb** suppresses toasts for every urgency while retaining
  eligible notifications in history. Turning it off does not replay old
  deliveries.
- Locking the session suppresses presentation and redacts notification
  summaries from status output; unlocking does not replay what arrived while
  locked.
- An expired or dismissed notification closes with the normal protocol
  reasons, and a sender's critical-urgency notifications persist until
  dismissed unless the sender asked otherwise.

### Quiet every application temporarily

Open the notifications button and enable **Do Not Disturb**. Turn it off to
allow new popups again. It resets when Pearl restarts and has no schedule.
Application sounds use the application's own settings.

For one application, use a saved filter below.

### Filters

**Settings → Notifications** edits blocking rules through the shared Pearl
draft, so they activate on **Apply & save** without a restart. Each rule
matches on application name, desktop entry, title, body or urgency with
All/Any conditions, and either blocks a notification entirely or keeps it out
of popups while retaining it in history. A master switch pauses filtering
while keeping your rules, **Test filters** evaluates a manually entered
sample against the current draft without sending anything, and blocked
content never appears in counts, flyouts or status output. Filter edits are
not retroactive: rules apply to future arrivals, not to existing history. The
draft workflow is described on the [Settings page](/pearl/docs/settings/).

### Quiet one application and keep its history

1. Open **Settings → Notifications**, enable filters and select **Add filter**.
2. Name the filter and choose **History only — no popup**. Add an
   **Application name** condition using **Is exactly** and the name sent by the
   application. This may differ from its window title.
3. Select **Save rule**. Under **Test filters**, enter a matching sample and
   press **Test sample**. Expect **History only — no popup**.
4. Select **Apply & save**. With Do Not Disturb off, request a new notification
   from the application and check that it appears only in history.

Choose **Block — hide everywhere** to hide both popups and history. Block takes
priority when several filters match, including for critical notifications.
**Is exactly** and **Contains** match literal text, without regular expressions
or schedules. **Match all conditions** requires every condition;
**Match any condition** requires one.

To undo a filter, disable it and **Apply & save**. Test with a new notification:
old history is unchanged, and blocked deliveries cannot be recovered. Saved
filters survive a restart; Do Not Disturb and history do not.

### Notifications unavailable or missing

Check `pearlctl session status`, Do Not Disturb, filters and whether the session
is locked.

If another daemon owns `org.freedesktop.Notifications`, Pearl waits for it to
release the name. Check the other shell or notification service's startup
settings through your distribution's service tools. Restarting Pearl cannot
release a name owned by another process.

## Tray

The tray widget shows StatusNotifier items from your applications. The bar
displays at most four icons, two on compact or vertical bars, and an overflow
button opens the rest in an expanded tray. Items render their themed or
supplied icons, attention and passive states, and tooltips.

- Left click activates an item, or opens its menu for menu-only items.
- Right click opens the context menu; middle click sends the secondary
  activation. Scroll events reach the application too.
- In the expanded tray, Space or Enter activates the focused item and Menu or
  Shift+F10 opens its context menu.
- Menus support nested submenus, separators, disabled and hidden entries and
  check/radio toggle state, and Back returns from a submenu to its parent.

Pearl cooperates with other desktop components here: it queues for the tray
watcher name without replacing an existing owner, registering as a host and
tracking items when another watcher runs. Likewise, when another notification
daemon owns the bus, Pearl reports notifications as unavailable until the name
becomes free.

### An application stays open after closing its window

Use **Quit** or **Exit** in the application's tray menu, or change its own
“close to tray” setting. Closing a window may leave the application running;
removing Pearl's tray widget only hides its icon.

For an application that returns at login, check its start-at-login setting and
[session startup entries](/pearl/docs/desktop/#start-an-application-at-login).

For a missing icon, check the tray widget and overflow list first. Pearl supports
StatusNotifier items and DBusMenu. Legacy XEmbed-only icons need external
compatibility support.

## Media

The media button, or **Media controls** in the compact Overview, opens the
media panel. It tracks up to eight players at once, with a chooser to select
one and real previous/next, play/pause, stop, progress and Seek controls;
anything a player does not support is disabled rather than hidden. The
progress bar extrapolates from the player's reported position and refreshes
once per second while the panel is open and playing, and an edited slider
position survives moving focus to the Seek button until the track changes.

Start playback, open the media panel, choose the player and press **Play/Pause**
or **Next**. If no player appears, check that the application supports MPRIS.
For a silent player, see [Sound troubleshooting](/pearl/docs/audio-power/#no-sound-missing-microphone-or-bluetooth-audio).

Album artwork is loaded from local PNG and JPEG files only. Network artwork,
other formats and oversized or invalid images fall back to a media icon; Pearl
never fetches network resources for artwork.

## Command line

```sh
pearlctl notifications toggle
pearlctl media toggle
pearlctl tray toggle
pearlctl session status
pearlctl session action --command dnd_on
pearlctl session action --command dnd_off
pearlctl session action --command clear_history
pearlctl session action --command dismiss --notification 42
pearlctl session action --command invoke --notification 42 --text default
pearlctl session action --command select --generation 7
pearlctl session action --command play_pause --generation 7
pearlctl session action --command seek --generation 7 --position 60000000
pearlctl session action --command tray_menu --generation 9 --menu-id 0
```

`session status` pages through notifications, players and tray items four
entries at a time; follow `next_offset` for more. Media commands also include
`play`, `pause`, `stop`, `next` and `previous`, and tray commands include
`tray_activate` and `tray_secondary`. Targets are identified by the
`--generation` (and for menus, `--revision` or `--menu-id`) values from a
current status call, never by labels or list positions, and seek positions are
in microseconds. Accepted mutations return a dispatch acknowledgement; later
status calls report the actual outcome. All session actions keep Pearl's
same-display, same-user checks and are rejected while the session is locked.

<Aside type="note">
  These services live on the session bus, and Pearl reconnects after a bus
  restart. Restarting the Pearl process starts an empty notification history;
  saved notification filters remain in your preferences.
</Aside>
