Pearl's sound controls talk to PulseAudio or PipeWire's PulseAudio-compatible
service, and its power controls talk to UPower, logind and, when installed,
power-profiles-daemon. The bar's audio and battery widgets open the compact
**Sound** and **Power & battery** pages in the control center, and Settings
carries the same pages in full. The battery widget hides itself when no
battery is reported, and a missing service leaves the shell and its panels
usable with an explicit unavailable state.

## Sound

The Sound page lists output and input devices with their volume, mute and
default selection, plus a row per application stream:

- Device volume covers 0 to 100% without amplification above nominal. The
  slider shows the loudest channel and scaling preserves the channel balance;
  mute is independent.
- Application playback streams can be moved to an output and recording streams
  to an input, including the corresponding default device. Their volumes adjust
  live. Read-only stream controls are disabled rather than misleading.
- Changes made by other applications, and your existing media keys, update the
  same rows; Pearl does not own the sound server.
- With no devices or a disconnected sound server the rows are replaced by a
  status message, and Pearl reconnects automatically when the service
  returns, without ever starting a daemon itself.

### Choose speakers or a microphone

1. Open **Settings → Sound**. Under **Output**, use **Change device** or
   **Use by default** to choose speakers or headphones. Do the same under
   **Input** for your microphone.
2. Check **Mute**, set a moderate volume, then start playback or recording.
3. Under **Applications**, check the application's volume and mute state. Use
   its **•••** menu to **Move to default** or **Choose device**.
4. Check the displayed route, then listen or make a short recording.

Changes take effect immediately. Applications appear only while playing or
recording. For player buttons, see [Media](/pearl/docs/notifications-media/#media).

### No sound, missing microphone or Bluetooth audio

- **One application is silent:** check its own controls and its row under
  **Applications**. For a microphone, check the application's input permission
  and any physical mute switch.
- **Sound uses the wrong device:** choose the default and move the application's
  stream to it. Check the route again after unplugging or reconnecting devices.
- **Bluetooth headset missing:** check for **Connected** in
  [Bluetooth settings](/pearl/docs/connectivity/#pair-and-connect-a-device).
  Use your distribution's audio tools to select headset profiles or codecs;
  Pearl does not provide those controls.
- **Audio service unavailable. Reconnecting…:** check your existing PipeWire or
  PulseAudio service. Pearl does not start a sound server.
- **No devices listed:** compare with the sound server's device list. If it is
  empty too, check the hardware connection, driver and device profile.

`pearlctl services status` shows the devices and streams Pearl can see; follow
`next_offset` for more entries.

## Battery

The Power & battery page shows the aggregate battery percentage, charging
state and estimated time to empty or full, and whether the machine is on AC.
The bar shows charging, full or discharge-level glyphs from UPower's state.
Firmware charge thresholds can leave a plugged-in battery pending charge,
without its percentage rising.

On a desktop without a system battery the battery card, source selector and
automatic power-saver row are hidden, the timers edit AC settings, and your
battery settings remain saved for later.

The page also keeps a session-only charge history of up to six hours, which
resets when the battery identity or service changes. An unknown charge or
health value is never displayed as zero or as "Good", and UPS or peripheral
batteries do not appear as laptop batteries.

### Check a missing battery or charging indication

Open **Settings → Power & battery** and compare its state with the bar. Add a
removed battery widget in **Settings → Bar & dock**.

If charging state does not change, compare `pearlctl services status` with
`upower -d`. If UPower also misses the change, check its service and hardware
reporting. Use the glyph and Power page to check charging; a charger-plug toast
is not guaranteed.

## Brightness

The brightness slider reads and writes the machine's backlight. Zero maps to
the smallest nonzero hardware value so the panel stays lit, and a permission
denial leaves the observed state unchanged. Writes from other tools, such as
`brightnessctl` or a brightness daemon, are detected and reflected in the
page and the on-screen display. A machine without a backlight device shows an
explicit unavailable state.

Pearl needs a supported backlight and a valid logind session. For an external
monitor without a system backlight, use the monitor's controls or a separate
DDC/CI tool; Pearl has no monitor brightness chooser.

## Power profiles

Where power-profiles-daemon is installed, the page offers its power-saver,
balanced and performance profiles. Only the choices the daemon actually
advertises are enabled, a degraded-performance reason is shown when the
daemon reports one, and the displayed profile always follows the daemon's
authoritative state rather than the last click.

Select a mode in **Settings → Power & battery**. If modes are unavailable,
check `systemctl status power-profiles-daemon.service --no-pager` and the page's
message. **Performance** depends on the hardware. Power modes are separate
from CPU scheduler profiles.

## Idle, sleep and automatic power saver

Open **Settings → Power & battery → Sleep & energy saving**, choose AC or
battery under **Settings for**, then set **Turn screen off after** and
**Suspend after**. **Never** disables a timeout. Use **Session & lock settings**
to set the [lock timer](/pearl/docs/security/#automatic-idle-and-sleep).

Brightness and power mode apply immediately. Sleep, dimming and automatic
power-saver settings also save immediately, without applying unrelated changes
from the shared draft.

The timers are constrained so the sequence stays sane: suspend requires an
earlier nonzero lock timeout, and screen off cannot be later than an enabled
suspend timeout. When dimming is on, brightness drops to 20% for up to 30
seconds before screen off and is restored on activity unless you changed it
yourself; dimming does nothing when screen off is disabled or brightness is
already at or below 20%. Screen off needs the compositor's output power
support, and automatic suspend locks the screen first and waits for the lock
to be verified; see [Session security](/pearl/docs/security/) for lock, sleep
and inhibition behavior.

**Automatic power saver** accepts off or a battery threshold of 10, 20 or 30
percent. While discharging below the threshold it selects the power-saver
profile, and exits after a five-point recovery margin or when AC returns. It
restores only a profile it still owns: if you changed the profile manually or
another tool changed it, automation stays out of the way until the low-battery
episode ends. It keeps running while Settings is closed and never rewrites
your saved threshold.

### Check sleep and resume safely

Save your work, then check that **Lock** and unlock work. Choose **Suspend** or
**Hibernate** in the control center's **Session & security** card and confirm.
Hibernate requires system storage and firmware support.

After resume, check that the session is locked, unlock it, and check brightness
and networking. Lid-triggered sleep follows
[system policy](/pearl/docs/security/#automatic-idle-and-sleep).

For an unwanted timeout, set **Never** for the affected AC or battery setting.
For a failed sleep or logout, keep the exact error and previous-boot logs
(`journalctl -b -1`). Avoid repeating a freeze with unsaved work.

## Power off and restart

Power off and restart are deliberate: the same button must be activated twice
within ten seconds, and the confirmation resets when you cancel, close the
popup or let it expire. The request goes through the system's normal
authorization, denials are shown rather than silently escalated, and an
accepted request is reported as acceptance by the session manager. No
power-off or restart command is exposed through the CLI.

## On-screen feedback

Volume and brightness changes show a brief card centered near the bottom of
the display that triggered them: an icon, the device name or caption, a
percentage and a level meter. The card appears for changes to the default
output from any source, including media keys and external clients, and for
any successful brightness write, but not for rejected writes or unchanged
values. Rapid changes coalesce into one card, a newer request replaces an
older one, and the card expires shortly after its last update. It is
click-through, never takes focus, and is dismissed by locking the session.

## System and scheduling

Choose a CPU scheduler profile in **Settings → System**, or add **CPU Scheduler**
under **Settings → Bar & dock → Add widget**. The widget needs an installed
profile file.

<Aside type="caution">
  Scheduler profiles apply to every user and persist across boots. Pearl's
  helper uses polkit authorization to replace `/etc/default/scx` and
  enable/restart `scx.service`. **Default** disables the service and returns to
  the kernel scheduler. Selecting a profile replaces hand edits to that file.
</Aside>

Pearl v0.3.0 uses `scx.service`, not the older `scxctl`/`scx_loader` backend.
It needs a sched_ext-capable kernel, scheduler binaries, Pearl's helper/polkit
files and a version-2 profile file. Pearl reads
`/etc/pearl/scheduling-profiles.json` before
`/usr/share/pearl/scheduling-profiles.json`; Git builds use `pearl-git` in these
paths.

After selecting a profile, wait for **Running** and the scheduler's identity.
**Custom** means a scheduler is running outside these profiles. If a profile
fails, Pearl reports the error and attempts to return to **Default**.

### Profiles installed but unavailable

Check the message in **Settings → System**, then inspect the kernel and service:

```sh
uname -r
systemctl status scx.service --no-pager
cat /sys/kernel/sched_ext/state
cat /sys/kernel/sched_ext/root/ops
journalctl -b -u scx.service --no-pager -n 80
```

`root/ops` can be absent when no sched_ext scheduler is attached. Check the
profile file and `/usr/lib/pearl/pearl-scheduling-helper`
(Git: `/usr/lib/pearl-git/`). Check your distribution's package instructions for
missing profiles, binaries, helper files or `scx.service`. A scheduler binary
alone is not enough.

When requesting help, include the Pearl, scx, scheduler and kernel versions.
See the [profile format and backend details](https://github.com/Seafoam-Labs/pearl/blob/6cb9b5526fd4998ccaee2fae3a9ceabdd52636f1/docs/SCHEDULING_PROFILES.md)
for administrator setup.

## Command line

```sh
pearlctl control-center show --page sound
pearlctl control-center show --page power
pearlctl services status [--offset N]
pearlctl audio set --kind sink --volume 45
pearlctl audio set --kind source --mute true
pearlctl audio set --kind sink --generation GENERATION --device INDEX --default true
pearlctl audio set --kind playback --generation GENERATION --device INDEX --target SINK_INDEX
pearlctl brightness set --percent 65
pearlctl profile set --profile balanced
```

`services status` pages through audio devices, streams, battery, profile and
brightness state four entries at a time, following `next_offset`. Simple
volume and mute changes target the current default device; targeting a
specific device or moving a stream needs the `--generation` and `--device`
values from a current status call. Accepted mutations return `queued:true`,
and the real outcome appears in a later status call, the panel or the
on-screen display. Audio, brightness and profile mutations are rejected while
the session is locked.

<Aside type="caution">
  The idle timers live in an `idle` block inside the preferences file. Older
  Pearl versions parse preferences strictly and reject it, so remove the block
  before downgrading.
</Aside>
